**What I found along the way**
- The `Ascend910B1` reports 24 cube cores / 48 vector cores, 192MB L2. On this triton-ascend build a `tl.dot` costs ~60–90ns regardless of size, so kernel time tracks `(#programs × #chunks × #dots)`.
- I built and validated a full chunked delta-rule kernel (in `work/dev/kernel_chunked_vp2.py`): in-chunk cumulative log-decay, `M`/`G` correlation matrices, exact 16×16 unit-lower inverse by the doubling identity, one state matmul per 16 tokens. It is numerically correct (matches the reference to `1e-4`), but its ~14 dots/chunk made it 46ms — no faster than the direct loop — and the `VP=1` (no duplicated work) variant exceeds the 192KB UB.
- Two backend quirks worth noting for future work: module-level constants must be `tl.constexpr(...)`, and a `tl.store` that reuses the pointer tensor from an earlier load is silently dropped (recomputing the pointer fixed it).

**Next steps if more budget were available**: the chunked kernel needs its per-chunk dot count cut (stack the `M`/`G` and `pred`/`out` operands, drop the cumsum dot) and a larger chunk with the banded/cross-factor decay treatment, which should land in the 5–10ms range.
